What Bedrock and Vertex actually charge for capability

Anthropic models cost the same on both platforms, so the platform decision is about catalog, not price. The spend decision is about something else: at an identical rate card, models differ tenfold in what a finished task costs.

Caching moves these numbers more than most model switches

Priced against the rates above, a 40-turn agent task resending a 60,000-token prefix each turn costs $2.26 on Claude Sonnet 5.5 at a 90% cache hit rate — and $6.00 with caching off. Grok's cache read is only a 75% discount, the weakest at the frontier; Claude 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol are 95%. Verify cache_read_input_tokens in production before trusting any forecast.

How every number here was produced

Method

Three kinds of number appear above, with three different standards of evidence. No model was run to produce this page.

Rates

Vendor figures, retrieved
How
AWS's pricing page renders from a live price map rather than static HTML, so figures were resolved from b0.p.awsstatic.com/pricing/2.0/meteredUnitMaps/{bedrock,bedrockfoundationmodels}/USD/current/*.json and the page's own templates re-rendered against it. Marketplace-billed models, which that map omits, came from the 134 cards under docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-*.html. Vertex from cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/pricing; DeepSeek from api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing.
Confidence
High, as of 6 October 2026. Several models here are under 60 days old and rates move.

Index and $ / task

Third-party measurement
What it is
The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — a composite of several independent evaluations — and AA's measured cost to run that whole suite at provider list rates, reasoning tokens included. Read off ~35 AA per-model pages. The benchmarks were run by AA, not here.
Not verified
AA's methodology, harness, prompts and repeat policy were not independently checked. Treat the Index as one third party's composite, not ground truth.

The caching figures

Arithmetic on stated assumptions
The workload
40 turns, each resending a 60,000-token prefix of which 90% is served from cache, producing 3,000 output tokens. Cache write charged once. Nothing empirical.
Weakest link
The 90% cache hit rate is an assumption, not an observation — the single most load-bearing input, and the easiest to be wrong about. A stale timestamp in a system prompt silently drops it to zero.
Reproduce it
Ships as platform-models.py; every parameter is a flag (--prefix --hit --output --turns --floor).

Not measured at all

Gaps
Not tested
Latency under load. Tool-use reliability over long runs. Long-context behaviour. Output quality on your workload. Whether caching actually engages for a given platform and model pairing. Rate-limit headroom.
Consequence
This page narrows the field and sizes the bill. It does not pick the model.

What this does not tell you

Limits
  1. One composite score. The Index says nothing about long-context behaviour, tool-use reliability, or latency under load — often the things that decide whether an agent is usable. Index 58 vs 52 is not a guarantee on your workload.
  2. The measured costs come from one harness. How much a model generates to reach an answer is prompt-dependent; your $ / task will differ.
  3. Catalog availability is not entitlement. Both platforms gate models per account and region. Verify access before designing around one.
  4. Prices move faster than this page. Re-pull before committing budget.
